Conceptual prototype · Built in Namibia

Glasses that turn a sign into a sentence.

SignSight is a conceptual smart-glasses prototype that recognises basic sign language and speaks it aloud in real time — designed to widen participation for Deaf and hard-of-hearing learners in mainstream Namibian classrooms, and beyond.

The problem

Classrooms are opening their doors. Not every lesson comes through.

Namibia's inclusive education policy has moved decisively toward welcoming learners with disabilities into mainstream schools. But policy intent and everyday classroom communication are two different things — and for Deaf and hard-of-hearing learners without a qualified interpreter nearby, a lesson can be present in the room without ever really arriving.

Interpreters are scarce

Qualified sign-language interpreters cannot be in every classroom, every period, in every school — especially outside urban centres.

Participation lags in real time

Written notes and after-the-fact support help — but they arrive too late for a learner to ask a question in the moment, alongside their peers.

Assistive tech is often imported, costly

Locally conceived, low-cost, offline-first approaches are rare — and rarely designed with a Namibian classroom in mind from the start.

How SignSight works

Four steps, running on the wearer's own face.

The concept is deliberately simple: capture, recognise, translate, and speak — entirely on-device, with no interpreter or internet connection required for the core function.

01 — Capture

See the hands

A miniature front-facing camera observes hand shape, motion and position in the wearer's field of view.

02 — Recognise

Match the sign

An on-device gesture-recognition model matches motion against a basic sign vocabulary — Hello, Thank you, Yes, No, Help.

03 — Translate

Form the phrase

Recognised signs are mapped instantly to short, natural phrases, ready to be spoken or displayed.

04 — Speak & display

Say it aloud

A bone-conduction speaker voices the phrase while a subtle HUD caption reinforces it visually, without blocking either ear.

Grounded in policy, not just prototyping

Built to walk alongside Namibia's own commitments to inclusion.

SignSight is a direct response to the direction Namibia has already set for itself — through the Sector Policy on Inclusive Education, the Basic Education Act, Vision 2030, and the National Policy on Disability. We see our role as helping close the gap between that policy intent and what happens, sign by sign, inside a real classroom.
