01 · Problem

Communication shouldn't be a barrier to learning.

Deaf and hard-of-hearing learners face daily gaps in classrooms and public life — not because they lack ability, but because interpretation is scarce.

430Mpeople worldwide have disabling hearing loss (WHO)
80%of school-age deaf children in low/middle-income countries are out of school
<3%of deaf children globally learn in a sign language they can use natively
02 · Solution

SignSight: glasses that listen with the eyes.

A camera, on-device AI, and a bone-conduction speaker work together to recognise basic signs and speak them aloud in real time — no interpreter required for everyday phrases.

  • Miniature camera
  • On-device gesture AI
  • Bone-conduction speech output
  • Classroom & everyday modes
Live interactive 3D prototype

04 · Challenge & Impact

Namibia's access gap is real and measurable.

Friction today

  • Only a handful of specialised schools (e.g. Eluwa Special School, Windhoek School for the Deaf)
  • Severe shortage of qualified sign-language interpreters nationwide
  • Mainstream classrooms often lack any interpretation support
  • Public offices and services frequently inaccessible to Deaf Namibians

Value created

  • Real-time basic communication without waiting for an interpreter
  • Supports inclusion in mainstream classrooms, not just special schools
  • Lowers the everyday cost of simple interactions (greetings, yes/no, help)
  • Complements — never replaces — professional interpreters

~8,300 culturally Deaf Namibians are estimated to use Namibian Sign Language natively (some older estimates of hearing-impaired individuals exceed 20,000).

05 · Competitive Advantage

Why this approach wins.

On-device first

Core recognition works offline — critical where connectivity is unreliable.

Hardware-agnostic vocabulary

Sign vocabularies can be updated per region/language without redesigning hardware.

Discreet, wearable form

Looks and wears like ordinary glasses — no stigma, no bulky external device.

Built with local context

Designed around Namibian Sign Language and classroom realities first, then scaled outward.
