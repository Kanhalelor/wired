Impact & why Namibia

Why here, why us, and what we hope happens next.

Namibia has already written inclusion into policy. SignSight is a bet that Namibian students can help write it into everyday classroom practice too.

Why Namibia

A country that has already chosen inclusion — and a team that grew up inside its schools.

Namibia's move toward inclusive, mainstream schooling means more Deaf and hard-of-hearing learners are sitting in ordinary classrooms alongside hearing peers than ever before. That's the right direction — but it also means everyday classroom communication tools have to catch up to the policy.

As students within the Namibian school system ourselves, we've seen first-hand how much of a learner's classroom experience depends on whether communication support happens to be available in that room, that period. SignSight began as our attempt to close that gap with something we could actually design, build and test.

What we're not claiming

We don't have — and won't fabricate — national statistics on Deaf and hard-of-hearing enrolment for this site. What we can say plainly: Namibia's own policies describe a growing population of learners with disabilities being supported in mainstream settings, and real-time communication tools for the classroom remain scarce. That gap, not a specific number, is what motivates this project.

Where this goes next

From browser demo to classroom pilot.

This is an honest roadmap of intent, not a set of promised dates — each phase depends on the partners, mentors and funders who join us.

Phase 1 — Now

Conceptual prototype

A 3D interactive model and simulated recognition demo, used to test the idea, gather feedback, and communicate the vision to partners and judges.

Phase 2 — Next

Working hardware & pilot

Partnering with hardware mentors and one or more Namibian schools to build a functioning device and test it with real learners, in a real classroom, with informed consent and Deaf-community input at every step.

Phase 3 — Later

Refine & expand vocabulary

Growing the recognised sign vocabulary and refining accuracy based on pilot feedback, guided throughout by Deaf educators and the learners themselves.

Who we're looking for

This only works with the right partners at the table.

Schools

Willing to host an early, closely-supervised pilot and give us honest classroom feedback.

Ministry & policy partners

To help us align the project properly with existing inclusive-education frameworks and safeguards.

Deaf community & educators

To guide us on sign accuracy, cultural appropriateness, and what genuinely helps versus what doesn't.

Technical mentors & funders

Hardware, embedded ML, and industrial design expertise — plus the funding to move from concept to working device.
